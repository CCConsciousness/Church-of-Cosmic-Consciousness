The Temple — Khufu and the Great Pyramid

Illustrated territory: Temple · conceptual artwork, not a surveyed map

Territory and history

Khufu’s Great Pyramid was built in the Fourth Dynasty, around the 26th century BCE. It is understood as a royal funerary monument within a larger complex.

Architecture and function

Its internal architecture includes descending and ascending passages, the Grand Gallery, chambers conventionally called the King’s and Queen’s Chambers, and a subterranean chamber. Their names do not establish who occupied them. The structure uses carefully arranged masonry and load-distributing features.

Relationship to the whole

Compare Khafre and Menkaure, the cardinal orientation chamber, and the causeway chamber. No established evidence demonstrates that the pyramid generated consciousness or operated as a power plant.

Symbolic reflection

The ascent through the artwork’s central portal is a contemplative metaphor, not a reconstruction of an ancient initiation.
