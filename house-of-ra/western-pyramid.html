The Smaller Royal Pyramids — Khafre

Illustrated territory: Western pyramid · conceptual artwork, not a surveyed map

Territory and history

Khafre’s pyramid is the second largest of the three principal Giza pyramids. Its elevated ground can make it appear taller than Khufu’s, and some original casing survives near its summit.

Architecture and function

Its complex includes a mortuary temple, causeway and valley temple. The Great Sphinx is commonly associated with Khafre’s complex, although its precise commissioning remains debated.

Relationship to the whole

Compare Khafre with Khufu and Menkaure. The artwork’s left-hand position is illustrative, not an identification based on geographic orientation.

Symbolic reflection

The pyramid form may invite reflection on stability and ascent.
